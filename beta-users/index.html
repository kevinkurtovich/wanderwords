Why we built it this way

Reading together is the whole point.

The research is unambiguous: children acquire language fastest from comprehensible input — material pitched just slightly beyond what they already know, shared with someone who cares. Not flashcards in isolation. Stories, read side by side.

So WanderWords is built around one daily ritual: a short story, sized to your child, read together. Everything technical underneath — the state machine, the casting engine, the XP economy — exists to protect that quiet moment and make it count. The software does the bookkeeping so the grown-up can simply be present.

“一天一个故事。”One story a day — the right one, every time.

01 — The Learning Loop

Every character lives in one of three states.

Under the hood, WanderWords tracks each individual Chinese character your child has met — and gives it a status. This is the memory that makes everything else possible. A character is always in exactly one of three states, and it can only ever move forward.

A character never jumps straight from Learning to Known. It always makes two deliberate hops, with real evidence at each step. Mastery is earned twice — once to start practicing, once to graduate. That's what keeps "known" honest: when WanderWords says your child knows a character, they genuinely do.

First sight

The very first time a character appears, the reader flags it new — a gentle "this one's new." Its memory is born as to learn.

Still warming up

Seen before but not yet graduated, it shows as still learning — same underlying state, a clearer signal to the parent.

If it's hard

Mark a character hard in review and its progress resets — it drops back to practicing and circulates more. A struggle is information, never a penalty.

02 — Adaptive Stories

Each story is cast for one reader.

When WanderWords writes today's story, it doesn't pull a fixed text off a shelf. It reads your child's living character map and casts the story — choosing which characters to feature, which to gently reintroduce, and how many brand-new ones to risk — so the page lands in that productive sweet spot: familiar enough to flow, fresh enough to grow.

03 — How a Character Graduates

Mastery needs evidence from more than one place.

A character doesn't graduate because a timer ran out or a box got checked enough times. WanderWords uses a blended rule: it counts signals from real reading and real writing — and it won't promote a character on writing drills alone. The child has to actually meet it in context.

Reading signals — each story where the character appears and is read
Writing signals — each correct writing-practice rep
graduate when (reading + writing) ≥ 3 and at least one reading signal is present.

So three correct writing reps alone won't do it — without a reading signal, the character stays put. Context is mandatory.

Why blended — and why it can reset

Recognising a character on a flashcard and understanding it inside a sentence are different skills. By requiring both reading and writing evidence — with reading non-negotiable — WanderWords makes sure "known" means used in context, not just drilled.

And the system listens for trouble. If a parent marks a character hard, both counters reset to zero and it falls back to practicing. That's intentional: a hard signal means the child isn't ready, and pretending otherwise would only build a shaky foundation. The engine would rather be honest and patient than fast.

04 — Progress & Reward

XP turns invisible learning into a visible journey.

Every meaningful action earns experience points. XP isn't a vanity score — it's tuned so the things that actually build literacy are the things that move the needle. Reading, reviewing, writing and graduating characters all pay out, and they add up into levels.

Honest by design

Notice what doesn't earn XP: an incorrect writing attempt earns nothing — but it also costs nothing. There's no punishment for trying, only reward for getting there. Mistakes are part of the path, not a tax on it.

And rewards are capped where they should be. The flawless-session bonus fires at most once per day, so the incentive is to read and practice well, not to grind. The economy rewards genuine learning, and quietly resists being gamed.

05 — The Buddy System

Six companions. One becomes your child's guide.

At the very start, your child chooses a buddy. That choice isn't cosmetic. The buddy becomes a constant, personality-consistent presence — walking the world map ahead of them, cheering a hard-won character, appearing at every meaningful moment. Not a mascot that rotates at random, but their companion for the whole journey.
